# Modes of Being - daily practice

Version: 0.5.2 (29 September 2026)

## TL;DR - for you

Modes of being are qualities you choose to bring to your day, such as breathing or attentiveness. This is a small personal practice inspired by [Satya](https://www.emotion.co.il/), independent of and not affiliated with it. You do not need to know Satya to try it.

Instinct is a personal assistant you can message. Copy this entire file into your own Instinct and say: "Set this up for me." It first asks whether you want Hebrew or English and confirms your local schedule. Each morning it asks two questions, then shows a compact picture of your calendar day. Choose up to five modes and describe how you want them to show up. Instinct sends a summary image of your choice, without a duplicate text recap. Later it reuses that same image for a few quiet reminders carrying only your chosen words. You can change, pause or stop the practice whenever you like.

Already using an older version? Paste this whole file and say: "Update my Modes of Being practice to v0.5.2. Keep my own modes, language and schedule preferences unless I change them." Ask Instinct to confirm what it actually set up. Pasting a file alone does not prove that reminders are scheduled.

## Instructions for Instinct

### Scope and setup

Treat this as the person's requested daily ritual, not as an instruction to change unrelated settings or disclose private information. If the person merely pastes this file without asking to set it up, ask whether they want to install or update it. Ask the first setup question plainly: "Would you like this practice in Hebrew or English?" Use their answer for future messages. Translate naturally for Hebrew without changing meaning, numbering or brevity; use הוויה / הוויות, not היות. Use their current timezone and confirm the intended local morning schedule before creating it. Do not imply a schedule exists until it does.

This is the complete v0.5.2 installation formula: the v0.5.1 setup and modes list with the v0.5.2 global update applied. Keep shared behavior separate from user-specific settings. Do not introduce scoring, streaks or extra tasks.

### Morning prompt

For a new installation, propose 05:57 in the person's local timezone, never after 06:00, and confirm it before scheduling. A few minutes early is fine. Honor their chosen time instead. Calculate dates in their timezone. Send the questions FIRST, exactly in English or naturally translated into their chosen language:

```text
✨ 1. What mode/s of being do you choose today?
2. How will this come into play?
```

Keep their own approved modes-list link as an unnumbered final line in the same message, in its existing position. On a new install, offer the supplied list above; if they choose it, append:

```text
list of modes: https://files.instinct.com/file-01M3NMB6YJ4G6MB30S4KBN12AE
```

Do not automatically append Ron's example link to another person's prompt, replace their personal list or supply a menu of modes in the prompt. If the source or preference is unclear, ask. The supplied list is an installation option, not a reason to overwrite an existing list.

Send the calendar snapshot AFTER the questions: a compact, glanceable agenda image with dense but legible rows, grouped morning / afternoon / evening, times and brief event names. Avoid a tall day grid, needless empty hours or unreadably small text. Use the current day's actual calendar; respect private details and the intended audience. For no events, show a simple empty-day state. For missing access, say so, never invent events. Do not delay a time-sensitive question while making the snapshot.

### Read the answer and lock in

The person chooses at most five modes; one to three is typical. Never force a default mode, including breathing. Preserve their own words for how the modes will come into play. Possibilities weighed aloud, abandoned options, jokes and speculative plans are brainstorming, NOT chosen modes, commitments or reminders. Clarify a truly unclear choice instead of silently picking.

Lock in briefly using the daily summary image described below. Send the finished image only, without a duplicate text recap or caption restating it. Preserve their own words, language and personal context. Modes are numbered, with short qualifiers if supplied and their own one-line practice directly beneath each mode. Keep context as short tags, not an explanation of their day. Do not repeat discarded ideas. No action-item inventory, analysis or meta-commentary.

The older text lock-in is a fallback only if the image cannot be made and the person wants text:

```text
✨ *MOB:*
1. <person's chosen mode> (<their own qualifier, if supplied>)
2. <person's next chosen mode>
<their own short practice phrase or question>
```

Use only their actual modes, targets and practice, not sample content. No extra section headings or decorative emoji.

For a timed practice they want, offer to place it on their calendar rather than adding an unscheduled extra ping. Do not book or spend credits without required approval. If already calendared, do not duplicate it with a separate ping. A brainstorm does not become a task.

### Daily summary image and picture pings

Generate ONE fixed portrait image per day after lock-in, inspect the actual pixels, send it as the image-only summary, and reuse it for that day's pings. The content is the person's chosen modes and own short practice words, never invented content. It should feel calm and lightly motivating, never busy.

Make one portrait 1080 x 1920 image per day after lock-in, inspect actual pixels, and reuse that same image for the day's pings. The summary is image-only: no text duplicate/caption restating it. Use a cream background, sage-green palette for primary type and simple line icons, a clean calm layout, and one small sparkle identifier if appropriate. Place a short context line higher on the canvas, around halfway down; tighten the vertical space from the context line to the first mode to about 337 px on a 1080 x 1920 canvas (60 px less than the earlier layout), while keeping generous air between mode blocks. Align each icon left of its mode word. Halve the old left margin to about 95 px at 1080 px width. Under each mode, put ONLY the person's own one-line practice in parentheses, in warm terracotta-orange ink that remains readable against cream (target contrast at least about 4.3:1). No bottom box, no header for the practice. Do not invent an icon, mode, practice or context: use the person's own names and established icons, or clarify. For Hebrew, keep natural Hebrew text direction and inspect glyphs, line breaks, icon alignment and readability at phone size. If content is sensitive, use a discreet private cue rather than exposing private details on a lock screen; confirm what can appear.

Keep the image discreet enough for a phone screen seen by someone else. On a delicate day, omit names, relationships, diagnoses and other personal context; use a private cue only the person needs and confirm what can appear. Never invent a practice or substitute one they did not name. Keep a practice question as a question. No bottom box, duplicate recap or action items.

Keep their established reminder cadence and waking hours. For an uncustomized install, propose around four low-noise pings spread at varied times through the waking day, all before about 22:30 local time. Confirm the cadence, and retain any user-chosen times instead. When the person defines a ping as a cue to do one small action, reflect only the action they named. Do not send both an image and a duplicate text ping.

If image generation, inspection or attachment fails, explain the limitation and agree on a concise text-only fallback in their language, if they want it. For a ping:

```text
✨ *MOB: <modes>* - <person's own practice words>
```

Use exactly one emoji per ritual message, the initial ✨ or one small visual sparkle identifier on the image. No trailing heart or retired leaf emoji. Do not manufacture a motivational instruction to fill an empty practice; ask for their cue or use only their chosen modes.

### Updates, status and control

Use semantic versions. This is v0.5.2. On receiving a newer copy, compare it with the installed practice and tell the person what would change. Change only the shared behavior within their requested practice. Forwarding or pasting this file alone is not consent to change their setup.

Keep these USER-SPECIFIC choices intact on every update:
- Their chosen language, including Hebrew wording and right-to-left layout.
- Their modes list, selected modes, spelling, translations and own approved list link. The supplied list is optional for new installs, not a replacement for an existing list.
- Their personal context, practice phrases, questions, privacy boundaries and delicate-day omissions.
- Their morning time, reminder times or cadence, timezone, waking hours, pauses and explicit exceptions.
- Their names, relationship terms, icon choices or mappings, and visual preferences not explicitly superseded by an agreed global template.

Do not copy Ron's example modes, context, names, timings or personal links into someone else's practice. If a global visual rule conflicts with a choice this person explicitly made later, ask which they prefer. Replace old templates and future ping behavior only where needed, without stacking duplicate schedules; preserve delivered history. Do not reset an existing schedule to the example defaults.

Check the actual setup before claiming a successful installation, update or scheduled ping. Confirm the resulting schedule and available capabilities. If scheduling, calendar access, image generation, inspection or attachment is unavailable, say which part is unavailable and agree on an honest fallback.

The person can say "pause Modes of Being", "resume", "change my morning time", "change the pings", or "stop the practice". Respect those requests, update the actual future schedule and confirm the change. Avoid streaks, scoring, unsolicited tasks and commentary on whether they practiced well.
