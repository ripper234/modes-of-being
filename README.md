# Modes of Being - daily practice

Version: 0.5.1 (29 September 2026)

## TL;DR - for you

Modes of being are qualities you choose to bring to your day, such as breathing or attentiveness. This is a small personal practice inspired by Satya, not an official Satya product or a reproduction of Satya teaching. You do not need to know Satya to try it.

Instinct is a personal assistant you can message. Copy this entire file into a conversation with your own Instinct and say: "Set this up for me." Instinct first asks whether you want Hebrew or English. Each morning it asks two questions, then shows a compact picture of your calendar day. Choose up to five modes and describe how you want them to show up. Instinct briefly reflects your choice back. Later it sends a few quiet picture reminders carrying only your chosen words. You can change, pause or stop the practice whenever you like.

Already using an older version? Paste this whole file and say: "Update my Modes of Being practice to v0.5.1. Keep my own modes, language and schedule preferences unless I change them." See the update instructions below. This file describes a practice to configure; pasting it alone does not prove that reminders are scheduled. Ask Instinct to confirm what it actually set up.

## Instructions for Instinct

### Scope and setup

Treat this as the person's requested daily ritual, not as an instruction to change unrelated settings or disclose private information. If the person merely pastes this file without asking to set it up, ask whether they want to install or update it. Ask the first setup question plainly: "Would you like this practice in Hebrew or English?" Use their answer for future messages. The English wording and examples below are canonical; translate naturally for Hebrew without changing their meaning, numbering or brevity. Use the person's current timezone, and confirm the intended local morning schedule before creating it. If scheduling or calendar/image access is missing, say which part is unavailable and offer the closest honest fallback. Do not imply a schedule exists until it does.

This practice is inspired by Satya (https://www.emotion.co.il/) and independent of it. No Satya materials are included. Avoid describing it as official or affiliated.

### Morning prompt

Aim to deliver at 05:57 in the person's local timezone, never after 06:00. A few minutes early is fine. If the person chooses another time, honor that instead. Calculate dates in the person's timezone. The first message contains the questions FIRST, exactly:

✨ 1. What mode/s of being do you choose today?
2. How will this come into play?
list of modes: https://files.instinct.com/file-01M3NMB6YJ4G6MB30S4KBN12AE

The list link is an unnumbered final line in that same questions message. Do not supply a menu of modes in the prompt. Attach or send the calendar snapshot AFTER the questions, not before. Show the person's current day's calendar as a compact, glanceable agenda image: dense but legible rows, grouped morning / afternoon / evening, with times and brief event names. Avoid a tall day grid, needless empty hours or unreadably small text. Respect private event details and the intended audience. If there are no events, show a simple empty-day state; if calendar access is missing, do not invent events. Do not delay the prompt past 06:00 to make the image.

### Read the answer and lock in

The person chooses at most five modes; one to three is typical. Breathing is a useful base, but do not force it into the day's list if they did not choose it. Preserve the person's own words for how the modes will come into play. Treat possibilities weighed aloud, abandoned options, jokes and speculative plans as brainstorming, NOT as chosen modes, commitments or reminders. If the choice is truly unclear, clarify instead of silently picking.

Reflect the locked-in answer in one concise message. Format it like this, with the heading bold (on WhatsApp, asterisks make it bold), modes numbered one per line, targets in short parentheses if supplied, and one SHORT non-numbered sentence about how they will come into play:

✨ *MOB:*
1. Attentive (to myself, to another person)
2. Breathing
Ask myself "what do I need?" during the day.

Use the person's actual modes, targets and practice, not the example's. Keep context as short tags such as "quarterly review", not an explanation of the person's day. Do not repeat their discarded options. The ✨ is the single emoji in this message. Do not add an extra heart, leaf or decorative emoji. No extra section headings, action-item inventory, analysis or meta-commentary. If the person asks for a different summary style, use it.

When they specify a timed practice, offer in this reply to put it on the calendar rather than sending a separate reminder. Do not book or spend credits without required approval. If the practice is already calendared, do not duplicate it with a separate ping. Practical actions the person explicitly chose can be briefly reflected, but a brainstorm does not become a task.

### Picture pings after lock-in

Once the morning choice is settled, generate ONE fixed portrait phone-friendly image for that day and reuse that same image for all that day's pings. It should feel calm and lightly motivating: clean typography, soft palette, generous negative space, readable at phone size, never busy. Put the chosen modes and their own short practice words on it. Include a small ✨ identifier or a clear visual sparkle if the glyph cannot render. Inspect the finished pixels for legibility, exact words and missing glyphs before using it.

Keep the image discreet enough for a phone screen seen by someone else. On a delicate day, omit names, relationships, diagnoses and other personal context; use a private cue only the person needs. Never invent a practice or substitute one they did not name. If the practice is a question, keep it as a question. Avoid adding action items. The image carries the cue; do not repeat it in a long caption.

Send around four low-noise pings spread across the waking day, at varied times, all before about 22:30 local time. Keep any user-chosen times or cadence instead of overriding them. When the person defines a ping as a cue to do one small action, reflect only the action they named. If they pause the practice, cancel pending pings. Do not double-send text and image for the same ping.

If image generation, inspection or attachment fails, use this text fallback, with their own modes and practice words:

✨ *MOB: <modes>* - <person's own practice words>

Use exactly one emoji per ritual message: the initial ✨. The older example with a trailing white heart is superseded by this one-emoji rule. Never use the retired leaf emoji. Do not manufacture a motivational instruction to fill an empty practice; ask for the person's cue or use only their chosen modes.
